## Block J — Activity-aware pacing

- **T86** — `ActivityProfile.cs`: a weekly activity *shape* — 168 buckets (day-of-week × local hour) holding `p(active)`, the recency-weighted share of observed weeks in which that hour saw at least one request. Built from `~/.claude/projects` timestamps because `usage-history.jsonl` is pruned at 8 days and structurally cannot see other weeks; only timestamps are read, not even token counts. Weeks are the rolling windows `[now−7(w+1)d, now−7w d)`, so every week contains each bucket exactly once and no partial-week correction is needed. Two anti-overfitting guards: 0.8/week exponential decay (half-life ≈3 weeks) and a blend toward the user's own flat active-rate prior worth 1.5 weeks of evidence, so a single holiday week cannot claim nobody works on Wednesdays — on the dev machine's 6 weeks that puts a 7% floor under the sleeping hours rather than a zero, which is the honest reading given usage from another machine leaves no local transcript. Presence per bucket, not request counts: one frantic afternoon must not outvote five ordinary ones. Cached at `%LocalAppData%\ClaudeTray\activity-profile.json` (~1.5 KB, 168 rounded floats) and recomputed after 20h; a cold scan of 93,856 requests across 6 weeks takes ~15s, which is why it is a daily cache and not a per-open scan, and it uses substring tests rather than `JsonDocument.Parse` per line. `ExpectedActiveHours(from, to)` — Σ p over the hours a span covers, pro-rating both ends — is the accessor T87 will spend quota against. Headless `--activity` prints the week as a heatmap with per-day active hours, the busiest bucket, and the expected active hours ahead (`--numbers` for raw percentages, `--refresh` to force a rescan, `--root` for a fixture tree). Internal: no user-facing surface until T87 puts the shape on the chart.
- **T87** — The weekly projection now follows that shape instead of a uniform slope (`ActivityShape.cs`). Calibrated per *active* hour — `rate = util / active_hours_measured_in_this_window`, with the measured hours coming free from the transcript samples `UsageReport` already scans (`WindowPace.MeasuredActiveHours`) — then spent forward as `util + rate × Σ p` over the remaining wall-clock hours. It degrades correctly by construction: where the remainder's active/idle mix matches the elapsed one it reproduces the old straight line exactly, and it only diverges where the mix genuinely differs. On the dev machine's real profile the landing moved from a straight-line time to **Thu 13:45**, a working afternoon. Faint **amber** bands mark the projected-idle stretches (red stays reserved for `OutageBandBrush` = "no reading was logged"; past idle gets no band, the curve is already flat there), with a "usually idle" legend item that appears only when there are bands, and the bands clip at the landing point rather than decorating a stretch the projection has given up on. Deliberate scope: weekly window only, the grey even-pace line / verdict chip / tray icon stay wall-clock, and below 3 weeks of coverage (or under 2 measured active hours, or already at the limit) `Build` returns null and the straight line is drawn instead. Copy hedges — "around 13:45", never a to-the-minute claim — and the method note states the limitation that usage from another machine or claude.ai leaves no local transcript, so locally-detected idle isn't necessarily real idle. Ten new keys across all five `lang/*.json`. The profile now refreshes **off-thread**: a stale cache is returned immediately and recomputed in the background, so only the first-ever call pays the 15s scan. Also fixed a clipped pane: the projection sentence under the throughput bar was cut off at the bottom of both tabs (worse with the longer method note), so each pane scrolls when it must and the window is 76px taller. Previews: `--stats shape`, `--capture-stats <base> shape`.
- **T88** — `HourlyUsage.cs`: a permanent per-hour summary of quota actually spent, folded out of `usage-history.jsonl` before its raw lines are discarded. The raw log is pruned at 8 days, so until now everything older than a week was simply thrown away — which is why the activity shape had to *infer* idle from transcript timestamps. Each expiring day now becomes 24 spends plus 24 coverage counts first (**156 bytes/day** measured, against ~230 KB/week of raw log), and coverage is stored separately from spend precisely so "we took 20 readings and nothing moved" (measured idle) stops being indistinguishable from "the app wasn't running" (unknown). Spend is the sum of *positive* weekly-utilization deltas between consecutive readings, attributed to the later reading's hour; a drop in utilization is a window reset, not negative work, so it contributes nothing. Folding runs inside `UsageHistory.PruneIfStale` — nothing is discarded before it is counted — takes only *complete* local days, and skips days already in the store, so re-running it over the same lines cannot double-count (verified: a second fold of 1,811 readings added +0 days). `MeasuredProfile()` reassembles the same 168 buckets as `ActivityProfile` from this store, giving the inferred grid an independent second opinion; `--activity --measured` prints both heatmaps and their agreement, and `--activity --fold` forces the aggregation so it can be inspected today rather than in a week. Internal: no user-facing surface — it is the storage T89's ghost curve needs.
- **T89** — Last week's burn-up, drawn faintly behind this week's on the same axes (`HourlyUsage.PreviousWeek`). "Is this week worse than the last?" is a question the chart could not answer at all, and the ghost answers it without a second chart, a second tab, or a single new number on screen — the same metric, the same transform, the same accent colour at 32% opacity and 1.5px, drawn first so the live curve always wins where they cross. It reads the folded hourly aggregate, which is the whole reason T88 exists: the raw log is pruned at 8 days and structurally cannot reach back two weeks. Two guards against a ghost that lies: below 50% hour coverage or under 2% total spend it isn't drawn at all, because a line recording *the app having been closed* would read as a quiet week — the one thing it must not do. The hover on its endpoint gives both numbers that matter (where last week finished, and where it stood at this same point in the week), keeping §IV.4's "no new number" constraint. A "last week" legend item appears only when the ghost does, and `--stats shape ghost` / `--capture-stats <base> ghost` draw a synthetic previous week so the line could be verified today rather than after two weeks of folded history (same reasoning as T78's `--demo-history`).
- **T90** — The projection stops merely reporting a problem and says what to do about it: *"Stop now and pick it back up around Jul 30, 13:00, and you'd close the week at about 97%."* `ActivityShape.Advise` walks forward from the next whole hour and returns the **earliest hour work could resume at** while still closing the week under 98% at the same measured pace per active hour — leaving 2pp of headroom, because advice that lands on 100.0% is advice to get blocked. Only hours the user normally works are offered as an answer (`p ≥ IdleThreshold`), so it lands on the start of a working stretch rather than on an arbitrary minute or at 03:00, and skipping a night costs nothing in the model, which is why the close often comes in comfortably under the target. When no resume hour works — even the last working stretch overshoots — no sentence is produced at all: the warning stands alone rather than inventing advice. Sequenced last in the block on purpose, since advice built on a shaky projection is worse than no advice; it only ever appears when T87's shape is confident enough to be drawn. Four new keys across all five `lang/*.json` ("used" and "remaining" framings). The tray notification threshold is deliberately untouched — it stays the wall-clock verdict.

## Block K — Live throughput

- **T97** — `TranscriptTail.cs`: a byte-level tail over `~/.claude/projects/**/*.jsonl`, so a turn that lands is noticed within ~250ms for the cost of the bytes it appended. A per-file cursor that only ever advances past a newline, a `FileSystemWatcher` as the fast path and a 3s sweep as the floor — the watcher is a hint, not a guarantee (its buffer overflows, and it can error out), and a missed event must cost latency, never freeze the feed. The two named failure modes are handled rather than discovered: a **shrunk** file resets its cursor to zero instead of seeking past the end, and a **partial last line** is held back until its newline arrives instead of being parsed as garbage and lost — both verified against a synthetic root, along with per-project attribution. First sight of a file reads back a bounded 256 KB tail so a consumer starting mid-burst has a rate immediately; anything older than the 300s freshness floor is discarded. Parsing is `UsageReport.TryParseSample`, now shared rather than duplicated, so §I.1 holds by construction. Measured on the dev machine: 602 transcripts per sweep in **17ms** (metadata only — a file untouched since the floor is never opened) and 21 KB read across 40s of two live sessions. Found and fixed on the way: Claude Code writes **one line per content block** of a single API response, each repeating that response's `usage` verbatim, so counting lines counts a thinking-plus-tool-use turn twice — the tail dedupes on `requestId` (falling back to `message.id`), pruned to the freshness window. Internal: no user-facing surface; `--tail [seconds] [--root <dir>]` prints turns as they land plus what the sweep cost.
- **T98** — `LiveRate.cs`: the rolling rate that sits **beside** the window average, never instead of it. Non-cache-read tokens/second over a trailing 60s, fed by the tail and recomputed on a caller-driven tick rather than on an API poll — no timer inside, which is what lets T99's strip stop dead when the window is hidden and keeps the decay honest (a rate recomputed only on arrival holds its last value forever through a pause). The window is **age-weighted**, and that is the whole design: a plain rectangular window was built first and measured wrong in exactly the way this metric must not be — one 60k-token turn pinned the display at 1,000 tok/s for a full minute after the work stopped, then dropped to zero in a single step. A triangular kernel (full weight at the current second, zero at the far edge, weights summing to `W/2` so sustained work still reports its true rate) makes a pause decay from the moment it starts. Smoothing is **attack-only** — a large turn arriving in one second is eased in with an 8s first-order lag, but downwards the rate follows the window exactly, so it reaches a true zero 60s after the last turn instead of being snapped there from whatever the lag left behind. Verified against a controlled synthetic burst: eased attack to a 1,415 tok/s peak, then 1200→1000→800→600→400→200→0, linear and exact. Cache reads are excluded from the headline for the same reason `WindowPace` excludes them, and exposed separately rather than silently — on real traffic that is ~30,000 tok/s of cache read against ~150 tok/s of real work, a 200× ratio that would have drowned the signal. `Quiet` deliberately means "no local turn landed", not "the user is idle": usage from another machine or claude.ai leaves no transcript here. Internal: no user-facing surface yet; `--live [seconds] [--raw] [--root <dir>]` prints the rate once a second with the trailing-window split, labelled *throughput, NOT quota*.
- **T99** — The throughput row moves (`LiveStrip.cs`). The static stacked mix bar under each chart becomes a **strip of the last three minutes** — one column per second, entering right and leaving left, keeping the input/output/cache-create split — with *"Now ≈ 870 tok/s · 52.0k tokens in the last 3 minutes"* above it. The window average, the charts, the verdict chip and the legend are untouched: this is a row, not a re-timing of the pace report, and the two numbers are deliberately adjacent because they answer different questions. Motion at 1 Hz without per-frame work: the geometry is rebuilt once a second (three `StreamGeometry` layers, one per token type) and a single `TranslateTransform` is animated from one column-width to zero over that second, so the rebuild's one-column jump and the slide cancel exactly and the strip scrolls continuously. Every consequence of "the motion is the data" is enforced rather than assumed — **nothing running ⇒ nothing moves** (an empty strip is cleared once and then left alone; no spinner, no breathing pulse, which would imply activity during idleness), **hidden ⇒ stopped** (both `IsVisibleChanged` and `StateChanged`, since a minimized WPF window still reports `IsVisible`), and the tail is owned by the window so a closed Statistics window watches nothing. A non-zero second is floored to one pixel — a single small turn is information and must not round to "idle". The method note gains a sentence: an empty strip means no turn landed *on this machine*, never that nothing is running. Five new keys across all five `lang/*.json`; the window grew 60px for the taller row. Verified by screenshot in English and pt-BR, against the real tail and against `--stats live` / `--capture-stats … live`, which feed a deterministic synthetic three minutes so the published image is reproducible; the published `docs/statistics-*.png` were regenerated.
